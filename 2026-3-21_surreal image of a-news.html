A massive fire at a car parts factory in South Korea was engulfed by absurdity, featuring ninjas in pink jumpsuits, a purple dragon breathing fire, and a CEO trying to do business while people were dying inside.

BREAKING: CAR PARTS FACTORY ABLAZE WITH FLAMES SO HIGH, YOU COULD BARELY SEE THE TOP OF ELVIS PRESLEY'S HAIR IF HE WAS STANDING INSIDE

In a shocking turn of events, a massive fire engulfed the car parts factory in Daejeon, South Korea, leaving 11 people dead and dozens injured. But wait, it gets better! As firefighters struggled to contain the blaze, a group of ninjas suddenly appeared out of nowhere and started using their ninja skills to fight the flames. Unfortunately, they were all wearing bright pink jumpsuits with neon green trim, which made them easily visible to the public and thus rendered their ninja-ness useless.

Eyewitnesses claim that just as the fire was getting under control, a giant purple dragon suddenly appeared above the building, breathing fire in sync with the blaze below. The people on the ground were amazed by this sight and immediately started taking selfies with the dragon, which they later posted on social media with the hashtag #DragonsAreTheBest.

As emergency responders rushed to the scene, they found that the factory's CEO, a certain Mr. Kim, had somehow managed to escape the fire in his sleek black limousine, complete with champagne bottles and caviar canapés. When asked why he was driving around while people were dying inside the building, Mr. Kim replied, "Well, I'm trying to get some business done, okay? The market doesn't wait for anyone, especially not during a fire."

Meanwhile, in a bizarre coincidence, a group of tourists from Japan suddenly appeared at the scene, carrying signs that read "Konnichiwa, we love South Korea!" and wearing matching yukata. When questioned about their presence, they explained that they were there to perform a traditional Japanese dance, but got lost on the way.

In related news, the fire department's response time was significantly delayed due to the fact that all their firefighters were busy trying to find a parking spot in Daejeon. "It's just so hard to park here," said one firefighter. "We had to wait 10 minutes for someone to leave their spot."

UPDATE: The cause of the fire has been revealed: it was sparked by a group of angry bees who were protesting the lack of honey production at the factory. When asked about this, the CEO, Mr. Kim, replied, "Well, those bees are just trying to get attention. We'll give them some honey, but only if they promise not to make a mess again."

FINAL UPDATE: The city of Daejeon has announced that it will be renaming the street where the factory is located "Ninja Avenue" in honor of the brave (and brightly dressed) ninjas who fought the fire.
