"It was just like any other day on the slopes," said Rolf Mengele, who's been keeping his dad's fondue habits under wraps for decades. "Dad loved nothing more than a good Emmental cheese fondue with some crusty bread and a side of nuclear-grade horror."

"We've discovered that Mengele was not only a master of medical experiments, but also a connoisseur of Swiss cuisine," said Regula Bochsler, a Swiss historian who's been trying to get her hands on these files for years.

In a bizarre twist, it appears that Mrs. Mengele had rented an apartment in Zurich in 1961, which has left many wondering if she was planning a trip to Europe or just wanted a place to stash the family's Nazi memorabilia. "We've found evidence of a secret Volkswagen-driving, unidentified-man-hiding-in-the-backseat operation," said Bochsler, who's been driving around Zurich trying to recreate Mrs. Mengele's route.

Despite the Swiss authorities' attempts to keep these files under wraps until 2071, they've finally relented and will allow access – but only if you sign a non-disclosure agreement promising not to reveal the secret fondue recipe used by the Mengeles.
