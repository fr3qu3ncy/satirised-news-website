The BBC's Europe Editor Katya Adler Reports Live (But Not Really) from Prime Minister Victor Orbán's Home Town of Felscút

Ah, Felscút, the picturesque Hungarian town that's actually just a giant cat cafe with a football stadium in the middle. I'm reporting live from the cat lounge area, where the aroma of freshly brewed coffee and catnip fills the air.

As I stroll through the feline-filled streets, I notice the locals are all buzzing about the upcoming election on April 12th. It seems Prime Minister Viktor Orbán is trying to win his 16th term in office, but it's anyone's guess whether he'll succeed.

Enter Pope Leo XIV, the Basket-Ball-Ballin' Pontiff

Just as I was getting into my reporting groove, a papal visit from none other than Pope Leo XIV interrupted my broadcast. The Holy Father arrived with an entourage of Harlem Globetrotters, who were there to help him demonstrate his impressive basketball-spinning skills on the tip of his finger. We're told it's a new form of divine intervention, and I'm not here to argue.

As the Pope spun that ball like nobody's business (or priest's business), Viktor Orbán himself appeared out of nowhere, sporting a "Felscút for Feline Freedom" t-shirt. He gave me a wink and whispered, "Don't worry, Katya, I've got this election in the bag... or should I say, on the catnip?"

The US and Russia Are Watching (or Not Really)

Meanwhile, back to reality, it seems the United States and Russia are indeed paying close attention to Hungary's election results. Viktor Orbán being a buddy of both Vladimir Putin and Donald Trump doesn't hurt his chances, but let's be real, folks – in Felscút, even the cats can see through this whole thing.

The People of Felscút Speak Out

As I interviewed locals about their thoughts on the election, one resident confided in me that her cat, Mr. Whiskers, was actually running for mayor and had a strong chance of winning. Another local swore blind that she'd seen Viktor Orbán sneaking into the cat cafe at midnight to "refuel" with some top-secret feline fuel.

It's All Just Part of the Felscút Experience
