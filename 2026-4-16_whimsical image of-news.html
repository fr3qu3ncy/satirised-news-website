SOUTHERN INDIAN LEADERS URGE MASS MOBILISATION... BUT IT'S NOT FOR WOMEN'S RIGHTS, IT'S FOR MORE PIZZA

In a shocking turn of events, southern Indian leaders have called for mass mobilisation over concerns about redrawing electoral boundaries. But what they're really worried about is not being able to get their hands on more pizza.

According to sources close to the government, the leaders of Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka, Kerala, and Telangana are up in arms because they think the new delimitation plan will leave them with fewer parliamentary seats. And you know what that means... less votes to order a large pepperoni pizza from Domino's.

"It's a conspiracy!" shouted MK Stalin, Chief Minister of Tamil Nadu. "They're trying to take away our seats and our right to eat as much pizza as we want!"

The government has proposed a new constitutional amendment to reserve 33% of parliamentary seats for women, but southern leaders are having none of it. "We don't care about women's rights," said Stalin. "What we care about is getting more pizza to the people."

Meanwhile, Prime Minister Narendra Modi has cast the proposal as a historic leap forward for women's empowerment. But when asked if he had any plans to reserve seats for pizza enthusiasts, he simply chuckled and said, "Ah, no... but maybe some extra cheese?"

PANDEMONIUM ERUPTS IN PARLIAMENT

As the debate raged on in parliament, things got out of hand. MPs began arguing over who would get to eat the last slice of pizza from the parliamentary cafeteria.

"I saw it first!" shouted a BJP MP.

"No way, I'm eating that whole pie by myself!" retorted an opposition leader.

The situation escalated until a group of Tamil Nadu MLAs burst into the parliament hall, waving black flags and shouting "No more pizza cuts!"

MODI CALLS FOR CALM... BUT IT'S TOO LATE

Prime Minister Modi tried to calm things down, but it was too late. The damage had been done. The parliamentary cafeteria was trashed, and a crate of pizzas was left on the floor.

As the news broke, Domino's pizza stores across India reported a surge in sales. People were buying up pies like they were going out of style.

"It's chaos," said a Domino's spokesperson. "We can't keep up with demand. But hey, at least everyone's happy... except the southern Indian leaders, that is."

THE PEOPLE DEMAND MORE PIZZA

In a bizarre turn of events, people began taking to the streets, demanding more pizza for all.

"We want equality!" chanted protesters in Mumbai. "We want extra cheese!"

As the situation spiralled out of control, Modi was seen fleeing parliament, carrying a large pepperoni pizza box.

"It's not my fault," he said, as he was chased by a mob of pizza enthusiasts. "I'm just trying to keep the peace... and my stomach full."

THE END

And that's how it happened. The southern Indian leaders' demand for more pizza led to chaos in parliament, and ultimately, to a national crisis.

As one MP said, "It's not about women's rights or electoral boundaries anymore. It's all about the pizza."
