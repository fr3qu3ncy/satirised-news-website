BREAKING: Robert F Kennedy Jr's Vaccine Tantrum Ends in 3-Hour Hearing

WASHINGTON D.C. - In a shocking turn of events, US Health Secretary Robert F Kennedy Jr was grilled by lawmakers yesterday during a three-hour hearing that was more like a wrestling match than a serious discussion about public health.

Kennedy, a longtime vaccine sceptic and cousin of the late President John F Kennedy (also known as JFK... or "Just For Kicks" to his friends), tried to shift focus away from his vaccine stances and onto "ending the era of federal policies that fuelled the chronic disease epidemic" in the US. But it was like trying to get a cat to take a bath - not happening.

Lawmakers, armed with facts and figures, pressed Kennedy on his handling of the worst measles outbreak in decades. Democrats from the House Ways & Means Committee accused him of bungling the response to measles and undermining the safety of childhood immunizations. It was like they were trying to get blood from a stone (but without the whole "stone" thing).

Kennedy, meanwhile, seemed more interested in talking about how President Trump and he are "challenging the status quo and the institutions that defend it as we work to make America healthy again in just 15 months." Yeah, because nothing says "healthy" like a 12.5% decrease in budget for the Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) staff and cancer research.

At one point, Kennedy was asked if he thought vaccines were safe and effective. His response? "It's possible, certainly." Um, thanks, Bob... we think you meant to say something else there...

California Democratic Representative Mike Thompson even brought up the fact that nearly 4,000 measles cases were reported in the US in 2025 and 2026 - and Kennedy just kind of shrugged it off like it was nothing.

But what really took the cake was when Utah Republican Blake Moore, who has a neurodivergent son, asked Kennedy about autism research. Kennedy seemed to think that taking Tylenol (paracetamol) during pregnancy could cause autism... which is totally not supported by any medical experts. Yeah, because nothing says "autism awareness" like making up fake facts and scaring pregnant women into thinking their baby might be born with a neurological disorder.

All in all, it was a pretty epic fail for Kennedy and the Trump administration. They must have thought they could just "Make America Healthy Again" by making a bunch of crazy changes to vaccine policies without actually doing any real research or listening to experts. Well, newsflash: that didn't quite work out as planned.

As one lawmaker quipped, "It's like trying to make a cat do tricks for treats - it's not gonna happen."
