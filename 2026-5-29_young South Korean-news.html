"CALL OF DUTY JUST GOT A WHOLE LOT KARDASHIAN-ER..."

The next Call of Duty game is set in North Korea and features "military authenticity" while somehow also fitting in Kim Kardashian's drama, because why not?

CONTROVERSIAL KIM Kardashian Setting for Next Call of Duty Game

In a move that's got everyone talking, developer Infinity Ward has announced that the next Call of Duty game will feature a setting that's about as surprising as Kim Kardashian at a family dinner: North Korea.

That's right, folks. The latest installment in the beloved series will have players fighting against an invasion of, you guessed it, North Korea. Because what's more fun than running away from Kim Jong-un's army while simultaneously trying to keep up with the Kardashians' latest reality TV drama?

According to sources close to the game (read: a guy who played the beta and got a free Xbox), the game will feature "military authenticity" - which is just code for "we're going to try really hard not to get sued by actual soldiers".

And if you thought that was all, wait till you hear this: the game's trailer features a group of young South Korean conscripts on a routine patrol... before they're suddenly attacked by North Korea! It's like they took every trope from a bad action movie and mashed them together into one glorious mess.

But don't worry, fans of Captain Price - he's back in all his gruff, mustachioed glory. Because what's a Call of Duty game without some dude yelling at you to "get behind cover, kid!"

Infinity Ward also announced significant changes to gameplay, including revamped movement mechanics and more interactive environments... or as they're known in the industry, "we took out the button-mashing parts and added more shooting".

The game will launch on current-generation consoles, PC, and Nintendo Switch 2 (because, why not?). And if you thought it was weird that there's a new Call of Duty game coming out without any actual PlayStation or Xbox logos on the trailer... well, that's just because Sony and Microsoft are still trying to recover from the last time Infinity Ward tried to "borrow" their console names.

So, get ready for another thrilling installment in the Call of Duty series - this time with a dash of North Korean flair! Because what's more American than invading an entire country while simultaneously trying to ignore the fact that we've been bombing them for decades?

UPDATE: The BBC has reached out to Infinity Ward for comment on how much it paid to get Kim Kardashian to do a cameo in the game, but so far no one is talking.
