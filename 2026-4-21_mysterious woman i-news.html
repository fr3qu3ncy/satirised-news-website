BREAKING: Japan Ditches Pacifism, Becomes World's Most Bizarre Arms Dealer

In a shocking move that has left the world wondering if they've stumbled into a Monty Python sketch, Japan has abandoned its 80-year-old pacifist stance and become the go-to country for buying arms. And not just any arms, but the kind of lethal stuff that would make even the most seasoned action hero think twice.

According to sources close to the Japanese government (or what's left of it), Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi has made a bold decision to relax restrictions on arms exports. Gone are the days of Japan being a peaceful nation; now, they're like the cool kid in school who's always carrying a few "just-in-case" Uzis.

This new policy means that Japan can sell its military hardware to more than a dozen countries, including the UK and US. And what kind of stuff are we talking about? Oh, just the usual: tanks, planes, guns, and maybe a few rounds of exploding catnip for good measure. After all, in an increasingly severe security environment, you've got to be prepared for anything... like a zombie apocalypse or a Justin Bieber concert gone wrong.

But wait, it gets better! Japan's Self-Defence Forces (which is basically just a nice way of saying "we're not really defenders, but we'll try") have taken part in annual joint military exercises with the US and Philippines. And this year? They're joining the war games as combatants for the first time, which means they get to fight alongside their American and Filipino friends while wearing matching superhero capes.

China, on the other hand, is not exactly thrilled about Japan's new arms-dealing business. In a statement that can only be described as "diplomatic," Beijing said it was "seriously concerned" about what it called Japan's "reckless militarisation." But hey, who needs diplomatic immunity when you've got a good old-fashioned arms deal to worry about?

South Korea, meanwhile, has expressed its own concerns about Japan's new defense policy. In a statement that sounded suspiciously like a high school student trying to sound wise, the South Korean foreign ministry said Japan's defense posture "should ideally be carried out in a manner that upholds the spirit of the Peace Constitution while contributing to peace and stability in the region." Ah, yeah... because nothing says "peace" like a good ol' fashioned arms deal.

In related news, Tokyo's stock market has skyrocketed after the announcement, with shares of companies like Toyota and Honda soaring due to the increased demand for bulletproof windshields.
