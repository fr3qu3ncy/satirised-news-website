13 People Die in Indian Firecracker Factory Blast, But Let's Be Real, It's Not Like They Were Making Something Worth Dying For

Thirteen people died in an explosion at a firecracker factory in India's Kerala state, because making fireworks is clearly not worth dying for.

BREAKING: 13 People Die in Indian Firecracker Factory Blast, But Let's Be Real, It's Not Like They Were Making Something Worth Dying For

In a shocking turn of events (or not), at least 13 people have been killed in an explosion at a firecracker factory in India's southern state of Kerala. I mean, who doesn't love a good firework to celebrate the upcoming Thrissur Pooram festival? It's not like they were making something that could be used for more important things... like making Donald Trump's hair look even taller.

According to eyewitnesses (who were probably too scared to get their names in print), the blast was so powerful that it felt like an earthquake. I'm pretty sure the only thing that's going to be shaken up is the factory owner's bank account when he gets sued for all the lives lost.

The explosion occurred around 15:30 local time (10:00 GMT), which is just enough time for the workers to get their daily dose of caffeine and still manage to die in a fireball. Firecrackers were being made in temporary sheds near paddy fields, because who needs proper safety regulations when you can just make do with some rusty old sheds and a few fireworks?

The Kerala government has promised to investigate the cause of the blast, but let's be real, it's probably just going to come down to "oh, we didn't see that coming" or "oops, our firecracker factory was located in a place where people actually live."

In related news (or not), Prime Minister Narendra Modi has announced financial assistance of 200,000 rupees ($2,137; £1,582) for the families of the deceased persons. I'm sure that'll cover all the funeral costs and maybe even get them a nice new TV to watch the Thrissur Pooram festival highlights reel.
