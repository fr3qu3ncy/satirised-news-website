"Mum's Gone Mental": Malawian Martha Ongwane Finds Sanity with Help from Saint John of God and a Bottle of Gin

In a shocking turn of events, 33-year-old Martha Ongwane, once on the brink of killing her autistic daughter Rachael, has found a rare support group in Malawi that's not just about saving her sanity, but also about saving her gin collection. That's right, folks! This feisty mum has discovered that with the help of Saint John of God and a good bottle of gin, she can cope with even the most trying times.

According to eyewitnesses, Martha was seen pouring poison into a cup, planning to feed it to Rachael, but then she had a change of heart – or rather, a change of gin. "I told myself it would be better if she died because that would mean she would rest and that would be the end of our problems," Martha said, trying to hold back tears. "But then I remembered I had a bottle of gin in the cupboard, and suddenly everything seemed brighter."

Martha's transformation is nothing short of miraculous. With Saint John of God on her side, she's now able to tackle even the most challenging autism-related situations with ease – like when Rachael insisted on playing the kazoo at 3 AM. "I just smiled, handed her a cup of tea, and said, 'Darling, you're a true artist,'" Martha explained.

Meanwhile, experts from Saint John of God are working tirelessly to educate the community about autism. They've even set up an awareness session in a local community centre, where they teach people that autism is not caused by witchcraft (despite what some folks might think). Instead, they emphasize the importance of understanding and acceptance – and a good bottle of gin never hurts.

In a related story, Martha's husband has been seen purchasing an extensive collection of gin-themed merchandise, including t-shirts, mugs, and even a personalized gin-infused cookbook. When asked about his newfound love for gin, he simply smiled and said, "Well, when life gives you lemons, make lemonade. But when life gives you autism, make gin."

The BBC contacted Health Minister Madalitso Baloyi to request an interview about national provision for children with autism but did not receive a response – probably because they were too busy sipping on some well-deserved gin.
