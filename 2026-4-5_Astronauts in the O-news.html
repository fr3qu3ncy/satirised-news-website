"NASA's Most Epic Space Photos Ever Taken... or Not!"

Four astronauts on the Artemis II mission have managed to take somewhat impressive pictures of the Moon and Earth, but NASA is touting them as "history in the making" despite being taken with standard cameras and smartphones, including iPhones.

The Unnecessarily Dramatic Story Behind the Breathtaking Pictures Beamed Back to Earth from the Artemis II Astronauts (aka "The Most Epic Space Photos Ever Taken... or Not")

In a shocking turn of events, four astronauts on the Artemis II mission have managed to take some pretty pictures of the Moon and Earth. I mean, who wouldn't want to see a photo of our planet looking all upside-down and whatnot? It's not like we've seen that before in every astronomy textbook ever written.

According to NASA (because they're definitely not just trying to drum up support for their mission), these pictures are "history in the making" and showcase the Orientale basin, a huge crater on the far side of the Moon. Wow, I'm sure it's never been seen before by robots or anything... I mean, who needs robotic explorers when you've got humans with iPhones?

But wait, it gets better! The astronauts are using standard 10-year-old cameras (because who needs fancy-schmancy space equipment?), including a Nikon D5, GoPros, and even smartphones. Because what's a space mission without a few selfies? I'm sure the hashtag #SpaceSelfie is already trending on social media.

And let's not forget about the excitement of watching the Earth go by! The window inside the Orion spacecraft got dirty, so the astronauts had to be told how to clean it. I mean, can you imagine being that bored in space? "Hey, Bob, remember when we were kids and used Windex on our mom's windows?"

The crew is thrilled to share their observations with us, including a picture of the Sahara desert and South America taken from 142,000 miles away (because, clearly, they needed to show off their geography skills). Professor Chris Lintott from the University of Oxford has weighed in on this earth-shattering moment, saying that "the value of the images coming back from Artemis and its crew is artistic, not scientific." Yeah, because science is so overrated.

Meanwhile, India's Chandrayaan-3 probe captured some pretty cool photos of the Moon's far side in 2023 (because they were actually doing real space research), and China landed a probe on the Moon's far side in 2019 (because they're trying to win this thing).

So, what have we learned from these epic space photos? Well, for one, NASA is still trying to justify their existence by taking pretty pictures of the Moon. And two, astronauts can take selfies with their iPhones while in space! Who knew?

Update: After this article was published, a group of highly trained monkeys on the International Space Station sent a strongly worded letter to NASA demanding more bananas and better Wi-Fi. The astronauts responded by saying they'll try to send some bananas up next week, but no promises on the Wi-Fi.
