BREAKING: Sephora and Benefit's Desperate Attempt to Get Kids Hooked on Skincare Backfires in Italy

In a shocking turn of events, the Italian Competition Authority (AGCM) has launched an investigation into luxury goods giant LVMH, owner of Sephora and Benefit, for their "particularly insidious" marketing strategies aimed at children. Yes, you read that right - children! It seems these beauty brands thought it'd be a great idea to peddle anti-aging treatments to kids under 10, because, why not?

According to sources close to the investigation (okay, we just made that up), Sephora and Benefit's marketing team was seen sipping espresso and stroking their chins, trying to come up with new ways to get kids hooked on skincare. Their plan? Create a social media trend where young influencers would promote their products, while subtly omitting warnings for children.

And, oh boy, did it backfire! The #SephoraKidsHaul challenge took off like a Kardashian's Instagram post - kids everywhere were buying and showcasing their skincare products under hashtags like "Get Ready With Me" (GRWM... what are they even trying to sell these kids?!). Experts warned that these products can cause irritation, allergic reactions, and in some cases, permanent skin problems. But hey, at least the kids looked cute with their face masks on!

LVMH, in a statement, said they'd "fully co-operate" with the authorities (like they had a choice) but declined to comment further, citing the ongoing investigation (which is just code for "we're totally busted"). The AGCM, however, was not having it. They released a statement saying that LVMH's actions may have fueled so-called "cosmeticorexia" - an unhealthy obsession with skincare in young people.

Meanwhile, Italian authorities raided Sephora and Benefit's headquarters, confiscating all the leftover face masks and serums (we're guessing they'll be sold on eBay soon). The investigation centers around possible unfair commercial practices linked to the premature use of adult cosmetics.

What's next?

We predict a PR crisis for Sephora and Benefit. Kids will stop using their skincare products (for now), influencers will delete their "SephoraKids" posts, and the companies will be left with a bunch of useless face masks and serums.
