ABUJA, NIGERIA - In a bizarre turn of events, six people accused of plotting to overthrow President Bola Tinubu have denied the charges, but refused to sing "I Will Survive" in their defense. The defendants, including a retired major general and a serving police inspector, appeared at the Federal High Court in Abuja on Wednesday.

According to eyewitnesses, the court proceedings were interrupted when one of the defendants, Mohammed Ibrahim Gana, was seen trying to get out of his wheelchair to dance to the song "I Will Survive". However, the judge quickly brought the proceedings back under control and refused to allow the defendant to sing.

"It's not a karaoke competition," said the judge. "You are here to face treason charges, not to showcase your singing skills."

The defendants, who include Erasmus Ochegobia Victor, Ahmed Ibrahim, Zekeri Umoru, Bukar Kashim Goni, and Abdulkadir Sani, were accused of conspiring with one another to levy war against the state to overawe President Tinubu.

In a surprise twist, former governor Timipre Sylva, who has been named as a co-conspirator, appeared in court via video link from his home in London. He was wearing a bright pink wig and sunglasses, and claimed that he was "not involved in any coup plot".

"I'm just here for the free Wi-Fi," said Sylva. "And to get some tips on how to make a decent cup of tea."

The court proceedings were further disrupted when a group of protesting monkeys, who had been brought into the courtroom by a well-known Nigerian comedian, began chanting slogans and waving signs.

"I didn't know we had monkey business going on here," said the judge. "Order in the court!"

Despite the chaos, the defendants remained calm and composed, and continued to deny the treason charges.

"We are innocent until proven guilty," said Mohammed Ibrahim Gana. "And even then, we will still be innocent."

The trial is ongoing, and it remains to be seen how the case will unfold.
