The man who could kick a cobra into next week has left us, but his legacy lives on in the form of thousands of internet memes that have been passed down through generations of keyboard warriors. And by "passed down," I mean we're all just trying to one-up each other with increasingly absurd and ridiculous Chuck Norris jokes.

For example, did you know that Chuck Norris doesn't dial a number; he just looks at his phone until it starts ringing? Or that the only place where darkness is welcome is Chuck Norris's closet, because that's where he keeps his black belt? Yeah, we're all losers compared to him.

But in all seriousness (just kidding, I'm not serious), Chuck Norris was more than just a meme – he was an action movie star, a martial artist, and a human being. And to that, I say: R.I.P., Chuck. May your kickboxing skills be the envy of all the angels in heaven.

It all started in the early 2000s when a group of intrepid internet users decided to create memes about Chuck Norris. At first, they were just simple jokes – "Chuck Norris can eat a whole pizza by himself" kind of stuff. But as time went on, things got weird.

People started saying that Chuck Norris didn't just do things; he made things happen. Like, did you know that Chuck Norris once rounded up all the stray cats in Texas and put them into a team-building exercise? Yeah, it's true – they're still traumatized to this day.

And then there were the memes about how Chuck Norris was basically invincible. Like, did you hear about the time he got bit by a cobra and lived to tell the tale? (Spoiler alert: it didn't happen that way).

But hey, who needs facts when you have Chuck Norris?

The Legacy of Chuck Norris

As we say goodbye to our beloved meme-generating machine, let's take a moment to reflect on his legacy. And by "legacy," I mean the countless hours he spent making us laugh and cringe in equal measure.

We'll always remember Chuck Norris for his tough-guy persona, his black belt, and his unparalleled ability to make us LOL with just one glance at him.
