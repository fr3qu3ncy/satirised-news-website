BREAKING: Japan's Meteorological Agency Issues Warning for "Huge" Earthquake, Promising More Tsunami Waves Than a 90s Boy Band Concert

In a shocking turn of events, Japan's meteorological agency has warned citizens of an impending "huge" earthquake that will make the one in 2011 look like a gentle spring breeze. The agency, known for its accurate predictions (just ask the guy who got stuck on a train), issued a statement saying that another quake could hit at any moment, bringing with it tsunami waves as big as a Kardashian's ego.

According to sources, the new earthquake is expected to have a magnitude of 8.0 or higher, which is just a fancy way of saying "the apocalypse is near." The agency warned residents to evacuate coastal areas and head for higher ground, but not before grabbing their favorite snacks and saying goodbye to their loved ones (you never know when you'll be needing those emergency KitKats).

Meanwhile, in Tokyo...

As the people of Japan scurried about trying to prepare for the impending doom, a group of brave office workers decided to take matters into their own hands. Armed with nothing but their trusty staplers and a can-do attitude, they formed a human barricade against the incoming earthquake.

"It was like a scene from a bad action movie," said one witness. "They were all shouting 'We're not going down without a fight!' while waving their staplers in the air."

And then there's Hokkaido...

In the northern region of Japan, residents were ordered to evacuate and head for higher ground. But little did they know, a group of mischievous penguins had other plans.

"We were trying to get everyone to safety when suddenly, out of nowhere, these penguins show up and start doing backflips on the beach," said Chaw Su Thwe, a resident who witnessed the bizarre event. "It was like they knew something we didn't."

The Aftermath...

As the earthquake struck, thousands of people were forced to flee their homes and head for higher ground. But what's a little chaos when you've got a Justin Bieber concert to attend?
