CHIRAIYA: THE BIRD THAT SHITS ON THE WORST OF HINDUISM

In a shocking turn of events, a new web series has taken the nation by storm, leaving millions of Indians questioning their sanity. Chiraiya, which translates to "the bird that shits on your house", is a gripping tale of marital rape, misogyny, and the importance of wearing diapers in public.

The show, which has drawn an audience of millions (including my nan), follows the story of Pooja, a poor woman who gets married off to her husband's brother-in-law (because why not?). On their wedding night, Arun (the aforementioned brother-in-law) decides to take what's his and shag Pooja. When she confronts him about it, he tells her that marital rape isn't a crime in India, and therefore, it's perfectly fine.

Meanwhile, Kamlesh, Pooja's mother-in-law, is busy rearing Arun like her own son, despite being aware of his questionable character. She believes that women should be interested in cooking and housework (because who needs equality when you have a good recipe book?), but eventually comes to realize that she's been conditioned by the patriarchal society to think it's okay for men to treat their wives like dirt.

As Pooja navigates her way through this messed-up world, she meets a cast of characters who will make your head spin. There's Arun's friend, a guy named Sanjay Mishra (no relation to the actor), who thinks it's funny to wear a superhero cape and shout "I'm Bat-Shit Crazy!" every time he gets drunk. And then there's Pooja's best friend, a woman named Sangeeta, who's secretly a chicken and only eats fried rice.

The show's director, Shashant Shah, says they took care to ensure the men in Chiraiya are not villainous caricatures (but let's be real, Arun is basically a human-shaped garbage disposal). They also consulted with experts on how to make the show more realistic, including a expert on marital rape, a relationship coach, and a dude who's really good at making chicken parmesan.

As for Divya Dutta, the actress who plays Kamlesh, she says she believes that impactful stories like Chiraiya have the power to change the world (or at least make people question their sanity). She's been getting midnight messages and calls from fans who are inspired by her character's journey towards enlightenment.

In conclusion, Chiraiya is a must-watch for anyone who wants to see the worst of Hinduism on full display. It's like a train wreck that you can't look away from – except instead of trains, it's a family drama with lots of screaming and wailing. So go ahead, grab some popcorn, and watch Chiraiya – but don't say I didn't warn you.
