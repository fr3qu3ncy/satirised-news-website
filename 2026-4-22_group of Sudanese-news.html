"DISCO-MERGENCY: UAE-Backed Mercenaries Fuel War Crimes with Polyester Suits, Disco Fights, and a Mariachi Band!"

In a bizarre turn of events, UAE-backed Colombian mercenaries have been secretly supporting Sudan's paramilitary RSF group in their fight against the regular army, while also hosting disco dance parties to boost morale and possibly committing war crimes over who got to be the DJ.

The paramilitary RSF group has been fighting Sudan's regular army since 2023, and also secretly hosting disco dance parties in their barracks to boost morale. A new report by the Conflict Insights Group (CIG) reveals that a network of Colombian mercenaries backed by the United Arab Emirates (UAE) provided critical support to the RSF, including drone operations, artillerymen, and even a mariachi band to play at their parties.

The investigation, which used data obtained from tracking the mobile phones of the Colombian fighters, shows that the UAE has been secretly funding the mercenary network, which has been fighting on behalf of the RSF. The report also reveals that the mercenaries have been using commercially available technology designed to make advertising more personal to track their targets and find the best deals on disco-themed parties.

"We are making public what governments have long known - that there is a direct link between Abu Dhabi and the RSF, and also that they have an amazing collection of polyester suits," says director Justin Lynch. "We are not just talking about any polyester suits, but ones with flared legs and platform shoes. It's a sight to behold."

The report details a pipeline that showed the mercenaries present at various regional staging grounds, most significantly a UAE military training facility in Ghayathi in Abu Dhabi, where they were trained in the art of disco dance by retired Colombian army Colonel Alvaro Quijano.

"It was like a scene from Saturday Night Fever," says Lynch. "We have evidence that the mercenaries even named their wi-fi network 'Disco Inferno' and used it to connect to the internet while downloading disco-themed wallpaper for their barracks."

The report also reveals that the CIG tracked a phone from Colombia to Nyala, in South Darfur state, where it logged into a wi-fi network named "ATACADOR" ("attacker" in Spanish) and later connected to one called "Disco Frenzy". The device was then seen traveling to el-Fasher, in North Darfur state, during the time last October when the RSF took over the city after an 18-month siege.

While in el-Fasher, the device connected to a wi-fi network named "ATACADOR" and later to one called "Disco Frenzy", says the report. It adds that the CIG identified other devices associated with Colombian mercenaries also present during the RSF takeover.

The fall of the city was accompanied by mass atrocities assessed as war crimes and crimes against humanity by the prosecutor of the International Criminal Court (ICC) and described by UN investigators as bearing the "hallmarks of genocide". However, sources close to the RSF claim that the true extent of the atrocities was actually due to a dispute over who got to be the DJ at the disco parties.

"CIG assesses that the UAE-Colombian mercenary network bears shared responsibility for these outcomes," says Lynch. "The scale of atrocities and siege in el-Fasher wouldn't have happened without the drone operations, artillerymen, and disco dance parties the mercenaries provided."

In another bizarre twist, sources close to the RSF claim that they were so desperate to get their hands on some decent disco records that they even sent a team of negotiators to meet with none other than the famous Disco King himself, Don Cornelius.

"They wanted him to host a special episode of Soul Train from el-Fasher," says an insider. "But unfortunately, he was too busy hosting his own talk show and couldn't make it."

The BBC has asked for a response from the Emirati government to the latest findings, but so far they have not commented on the allegations of disco-themed parties or Don Cornelius's potential involvement.

As one RSF soldier put it: "It's all about the disco, baby. We'll dance our way through this war and come out on top."
