BREAKING: Canadian Woman Killed, 13 Injured in Bizarre Incident at Mexico's Teotihuacán Pyramids

Mexico City, Mexico - In a shocking turn of events, a 32-year-old Canadian woman was killed and 13 people injured when a gunman opened fire at the ancient pyramid complex of Teotihuacán. The shooter, identified as Julio César Jasso Ramírez, a Mexican national, took his own life after the attack.

But wait, it gets even better! Eyewitnesses claim that the gunman was armed with a digital tablet and shouted "I'm going to make this pyramid go viral!" before unleashing a hail of bullets. Witnesses also reported seeing the shooter wearing a bright pink tutu and a pair of inflatable shoes, which may have contributed to the chaos.

"This guy was like a Mexican version of Chuck Norris, but instead of roundhouse kicking bad guys, he was just shooting randomly into the air," said one witness. "We thought it was part of some sort of bizarre cultural ritual at first."

The British couple who were on vacation in Mexico and witnessed the incident described chaotic scenes, saying: "There was a big push and panic of everybody running at the same time to get out... It was like a scene from 'Zombie Apocalypse' meets 'The Walking Dead.'"

President Claudia Sheinbaum of Mexico has expressed her solidarity with the victims and promised to investigate the incident. In a statement, she said: "What happened today in Teotihuacán deeply pains us. I express my most sincere solidarity with the affected individuals and their families... And also with Julio César's family, because, you know, he was just trying to make a viral video."

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney has condemned the attack, saying: "This is a terrible circumstance... We're working with Mexican authorities to get to the bottom of this. And by 'bottom,' I mean, you know, the bottom of the pyramid."

The incident has raised concerns about security in Mexico ahead of the Fifa World Cup, which will be held in Mexico City in just seven weeks' time. Tournament organizers have promised to deploy nearly 100,000 security personnel, but it's unclear if they'll be able to stop Julio César's viral video from spreading.

In related news, the Mexican government has announced plans to add a new attraction to the Teotihuacán site: an immersive nighttime show for tourists during the World Cup. The show will feature pyramids, music, and dance, but not necessarily in that order.

Meanwhile, a young boy who was shot in the leg during the attack has been rushed to hospital and is currently on a ventilator. His condition is stable, but he's still recovering from the shock of seeing his first viral video get interrupted by gunfire.
