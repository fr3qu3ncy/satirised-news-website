Domestic Workers Win the Right to Not Be Totally Exploited in Indonesia

Indonesia has passed a law that prevents domestic workers from being treated like dirt, but experts warn it's just the beginning of a long fight against exploitation.

"Domestic Workers Win the Right to Not Be Totally Exploited in Indonesia"

In a shocking turn of events, the Indonesian parliament has passed a law that will prevent domestic workers from being treated like dirt. Yes, you read that right - the country's 4.2 million domestic workers, mostly women, were previously not even considered "workers" under local labour laws.

The new law, which was first introduced in 2004 but repeatedly ran up against roadblocks, will now require placement agencies to stop deducting wages from workers' salaries (because who needs a decent income when you're serving the boss's family?). It also makes it illegal to hire children under the age of 18 as domestic workers (no more tiny tots being forced to clean toilets).

The law was met with tears of joy from many domestic workers, including Ajeng Astuti, who exclaimed, "This is like a dream come true! We've been fighting for this for 22 years!" Others were less enthusiastic, however. "I'm just happy I can finally take a break without getting fired," said one worker.

The Indonesian government is celebrating the passage of the law by hosting a giant party with all-you-can-eat nasi goreng and free massages for everyone in attendance (because who doesn't love a good massage?). The festivities will also feature a performance by pop star Katy Perry, who has been invited to sing her hit song "Roar" in celebration of the new law.

Meanwhile, rights groups are cautioning that the struggle is far from over. "This is just the beginning," said Lita Anggraini of the Jala PRT rights group. "We need to educate employers about their responsibilities and make sure they're not exploiting workers even further."

In related news, the Indonesian government has announced plans to create a special task force to track down and punish anyone who tries to exploit domestic workers (aka all bosses ever). The team will be led by none other than Arnold Schwarzenegger, who has been hired as a consultant to help with the mission.

The passage of this law marks a major victory for human rights advocates and is expected to send shockwaves throughout the global economy. Stock markets around the world are plummeting as investors panic about the potential impact on labor costs (yay, finally!).

And in a bizarre twist, the news of the new law has inspired a group of hipsters to start a new social media trend: #NotExploitedYet. They're posting pictures of themselves serving coffee and doing chores without getting paid, with hashtags like "#ExploitationIsSoLastSeason" and "#MyBossIsAFreeloader".
