EX-HEAD MONK OF CHINA'S 'KUNG FU TEMPLE' JAILED FOR EMBEZZLEMENT, BUT HE GETS A FREE PASS TO PLAY VIDEO GAMES AND EAT PIZZA FOR THE NEXT 24 YEARS

A former head monk of China's Shaolin Temple was sentenced to 24 years in prison for embezzlement, but gets to spend his time playing video games and eating pizza.

BREAKING: EX-HEAD MONK OF CHINA'S 'KUNG FU TEMPLE' JAILED FOR EMBEZZLEMENT, BUT HE GETS A FREE PASS TO PLAY VIDEO GAMES AND EAT PIZZA FOR THE NEXT 24 YEARS

In a shocking turn of events, Shi Yongxin, the former head monk of China's famous Shaolin Temple, has been sentenced to 24 years in prison for crimes including embezzlement and bribery. But don't worry, he'll have plenty of time to catch up on his favorite video games, eat an entire pizza by himself (with extra cheese), and learn how to play the trombone.

According to sources, Shi misappropriated temple assets worth more than 282 million yuan ($42m; £31m) from 2003 to 2025. But hey, who needs that kind of money when you can spend it on a new Xbox or a vacation in Hawaii? The court said he also used his official position to illegally obtain millions from temple construction projects, as well as offering huge bribes to Chinese officials. Yeah, because nothing says "bribe" like a few hundred thousand yuan and a nice plate of kung pao chicken.

Shi, whose birth name is Liu Yingcheng (which sounds suspiciously like "I'm a Cheng"), had earlier admitted his guilt, China's state Xinhua news agency reported. On Friday, he said he would not appeal against the verdict, which was probably because he knew he'd lose and end up with a lifetime supply of Brussels sprouts.

The 1,500-year-old Shaolin Temple - located on a mountain range that's basically just a giant dust bunny - attracts thousands of disciples from China and elsewhere every year. Shi took office there as abbot in 1999, soon earning the nickname "CEO monk" for transforming the institution into a global brand...of questionable accounting practices.

Under his leadership, the temple started opening schools outside China and formed a travelling troupe of monks who performed Shaolin kung fu shows - the temple's signature style of martial arts. But let's be real, it was basically just a bunch of guys in orange robes flailing around on stage while trying to hit each other with sticks.

Last year he was defrocked (which is like being kicked out of a monastery, but without the whole "having to leave" thing), China's Buddhist association said. Shi was investigated for embezzlement and fathering several children in 2015, but was later cleared of the charges...or so we thought.

In an interview with BBC Chinese that year, he said: "If there were a problem, it would have surfaced long ago." Yeah, because nothing says "problem" like getting caught embezzling millions and bribing officials. The name "Shaolin Temple" has gained prominence in pop culture over the years, including being the title of a 1982 film starring Jet Li...who's probably still trying to figure out what happened to his money.

UPDATE: Shi has been spotted at the local arcade, playing games like "Pac-Man" and "Donkey Kong" with his fellow inmates. When asked about his sentence, he simply shrugged and said, "No big deal, I'll just appeal...to my Xbox controller."
