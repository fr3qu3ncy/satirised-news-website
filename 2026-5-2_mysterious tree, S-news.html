ORAL HISTORY TRACES A FAMILY'S ROOTS TO A TREE PLANTED IN THE 13TH CENTURY BY DARTH VADER

In a shocking revelation that has left historians scrambling for their lightsabers, it has been discovered that the roots of a family in Ghana can be traced back to a tree planted by none other than Darth Vader himself in the 13th century.

According to oral history passed down through generations (or so they claim), Darth Vader, disguised as a Fanti warrior named Komfo Nana Asumbia, planted a tree in the fishing town of Apam. This tree, known as Santseo, has been standing tall for over seven centuries and is said to be a direct descendant of the original tree that Vader (or Asumbia) planted.

Eyewitnesses claim that they have seen Darth Vader himself walking around the tree, muttering something about "bringing balance to the Force" while sipping on a cup of tea. Local residents have also reported strange occurrences, such as lightsaber-wielding children and Jedi mind tricks being used to convince them to buy more fish from the local market.

"We were just sitting under Santseo one day when suddenly, Darth Vader appeared out of nowhere," said 97-year-old resident, Agnes Boateng. "He was like, 'You will do my bidding,' and we're all thinking, 'Uh, no, you will not.' But then he gave us some nice tea and a sandwich, so I guess it was worth it."

Despite the incredible claims made by the family and local residents, experts are skeptical about the validity of Darth Vader's involvement in planting Santseo. "I mean, come on, it's just a tree," said Dr. Jane Thompson, a leading expert in Ghanaian history. "And what makes you think that Darth Vader was even there? It's not like he had a Facebook account or anything."

However, the family remains adamant that their oral history is true and that Santseo holds the key to understanding the mysterious connection between Ghana and the Galactic Empire.

"We've always known that our ancestors were close to the Sith," said Mark Wilberforce, the current caretaker of Santseo. "And now we have proof! Well, sort of."

Meanwhile, in a bizarre twist, it has been revealed that Fort Patience, the 17th-century Dutch trading fort located next to Santseo, was actually built by none other than Ewoks, using materials scavenged from the set of Star Wars.

"Yes, you heard me right," said Apam's mayor. "Those little furry creatures were quite handy with a hammer and some sawdust."

As news of this extraordinary revelation spreads, tourists are flocking to Apam to catch a glimpse of Santseo, the tree that holds the secret to Ghana's connection to the galaxy far, far away.
