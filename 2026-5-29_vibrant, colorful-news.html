ITALY RESTORES LUCKY TESTICLES ON BULL MOSAIC WORN DOWN BY TOURISTS... AGAIN

In a move that has left experts baffled, Italian authorities have once again restored the "lucky testicles" of a bull mosaic in Milan's Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II. Because, you know, people need to spin around and grind their heels on ancient art for good fortune.

As the legend goes (or at least, the slightly exaggerated version that gets passed down), tourists who perform this ritual are guaranteed to have a great day. And by "great day", I mean they'll probably just trip over their own feet while trying to get a selfie with the bull and end up with a nasty case of heel bruise.

The restoration process involves a team of expert craftsmen, armed with nothing but a bucket of glue and some spare pink tiles, who carefully reattach the mosaic's delicate testicles. It's like a game of " Tetris", but instead of fitting blocks together, they're trying to recreate an ancient work of art that's been worn down by thousands of clumsy tourists.

"We're taking care of it so that it continues to be... um, worn down?" said city councillor Emmanuel Conte, when asked about the restoration. "Yeah, that's right. We want people to keep coming back and grinding their heels on our bull, because that's just what we Italians do."

The last time this happened (which was, coincidentally, also the first time it happened), the restoration took exactly 11 years to complete. This time around, they're aiming for a speedy turnaround of... well, let's just say "less than 11 years".

In related news, the Italian government has announced plans to create a new national holiday: "National Spinning Around and Grinding Your Heels on Ancient Art Day". It will be celebrated annually, with festivities including (but not limited to) reenactments of the bull-mosaic-spinning ritual, followed by an impromptu game of musical chairs played on the Galleria's floor.
