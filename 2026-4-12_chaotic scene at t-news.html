In a shocking turn of events, at least 30 people were feared dead in a wild stampede at the Laferrière Citadel, a popular tourist site in Haiti. The incident occurred during an annual Easter gathering, which was attended by many young people and a surprise guest appearance by Elvis Presley.

Eyewitnesses claim that the crowd became unruly when Elvis suddenly broke into an impromptu rendition of "Hound Dog" near the entrance to the site. As the King's fans rushed to get a closer look, they became trapped in a massive crush, with some people getting trampled underfoot.

"It was like a scene from 'The Walking Dead'," said one witness, who wished to remain anonymous. "People were screaming and running everywhere. I saw a guy wearing a 'Tupac Lives' t-shirt get squished by a group of partygoers. It was absolute pandemonium."

The stampede was exacerbated by the onset of heavy rain, which caused the crowd to become even more frenzied. Witnesses claim that someone shouted "They're here! The aliens are coming!" as the crowd surged forward.

Haiti's Prime Minister Alix Didier Fils-Aimé issued a statement expressing his condolences to the affected families and promising an investigation into the incident. However, many are questioning the wisdom of having Elvis at the event in the first place.

"I mean, who needs that kind of excitement at a historical site?" said Jean-Henri Petit, head of civil protection for Haiti's Nord department. "It's like trying to have a picnic in the middle of a zombie apocalypse."

As the news spread, fans of the King took to social media to express their shock and outrage.

"Elvis would never intentionally cause harm to his fans," tweeted @ElvisForever23. "This is clearly a case of 'The System' failing us. We need more Elvis in our lives, not less!"

In related news, sales of Elvis's back catalog have skyrocketed since the incident, with fans rushing to buy up copies of his greatest hits.

Meanwhile, officials are working to determine how many people were actually killed during the stampede. As one source put it, "It's like trying to count the number of hairs on Elvis's iconic pompadour – impossible and utterly ridiculous."

UPDATE: The death toll has now risen to 37, with officials confirming that the victims included a group of tourists from a Caribbean island nation known for its steel drum music.
