Breaking News: Robbery Gone Wrong in Naples

In a bizarre incident that has left authorities scratching their heads, a group of armed men robbed a bank in broad daylight in Naples, Italy. However, things took a turn for the absurd when they made their escape through the city's sewer system.

Eyewitnesses claim that the robbers, who were reportedly dressed in black and wearing funny mustaches, entered the Crédit Agricole branch on Piazza Medaglie d'Oro at around 10:00 GMT. They held 25 people hostage, including a group of schoolchildren on a field trip to learn about ancient Roman plumbing.

As police surrounded the bank, the robbers negotiated with authorities through a megaphone, demanding a ransom and promising not to use violence (except for one guy who kept yelling at the pigeons).

Just as the hostages were being released, the robbers made their escape by crawling into a manhole cover in the square. Witnesses described the scene as "surreal" and "like something out of a bad action movie."

"It was like they had planned it for months," said one bystander. "I mean, who needs a getaway car when you have a sewer system?"

As police scrambled to catch up with the robbers, a group of firefighters arrived on the scene, armed with battering rams and wearing Batman costumes.

"We were just trying to help," said Firefighter #1. "But then we realized it was a robbery, so we put on our best 'I'm not involved' faces."

Meanwhile, a team of special forces from Tuscany was deployed to track down the robbers. They arrived on the scene wearing camouflage gear and carrying what appeared to be toy guns.

"We were told they were armed to the teeth," said Special Forces Leader #1. "But it turns out they just had some really good makeup skills."

As the investigation continues, authorities are still trying to determine how the robbers managed to escape through the sewer system without getting stuck in the mud or being eaten by a giant rat.

In related news, the city of Naples has announced plans to improve its sewer system to prevent any future robberies. When asked about the plans, a spokesperson for the city said, "We're not going to let these robbers get away with it. Unless they can find a tunnel that doesn't smell like last week's pizza."
