"AUTHOR ARRESTED FOR POSSESSING CHILD EXPLOITATION MATERIAL... BUT IT WAS JUST HIS HAIRCUT"

Renowned author Craig Silvey was arrested for possessing child exploitation material - but only because his own writing about rural Australian kids had been deemed exploitative, not the actual children who were secretly writing their own bestselling books alongside him and his therapy kangaroo Blinky.

WARNING: THIS ARTICLE CONTAINS MILDLY INAPPROPRIATE LANGUAGE AND REFERENCES TO CHILDREN'S BOOKS

The Jasper Jones and Runt author was arrested in January during a police raid at his Perth home. Or so we thought. But little did the world know, the real reason for the arrest was because Craig Silvey had been caught red-handed trying to get a refund on his latest haircut from the Perth branch of Supercuts. Yes, you read that right – the acclaimed author was attempting to return his new look after discovering it made him look suspiciously like Dolly Parton.

As police stormed his home, they found Silvey frantically searching for his iPhone, which had mysteriously disappeared under a pile of dirty socks and leftover pizza boxes. "It's been stolen by my cat!" he cried, as officers seized several electronic devices that would later be revealed to contain copious amounts of cat videos.

In court on Tuesday, Silvey pleaded guilty to charges of possessing child exploitation material – but only because it was his own writing that had been deemed exploitative, not the actual children. "It's all about context, your honor," he said with a wink. "I'm just trying to bring awareness to the issues faced by young adults in rural Australia."

Prosecutors dropped two extra charges after discovering Silvey had actually written Runt as a tribute to his own childhood, where he spent hours playing with his pet kangaroo, Blinky. It turned out that Blinky was not only real but also a certified therapy animal – and had been secretly writing children's books in his spare time.

Since the arrest, publishers have begun pulling Silvey's books from shelves due to their alleged exploitation of children's rights. Schools have followed suit, citing concerns over the potentially damaging effects on young minds caused by reading about kids' adventures in the outback. As one educator said, "We can't have our students getting ideas about having fun outdoors when they could be stuck inside playing Fortnite instead."

The BBC has learned that Silvey's next novel, Rhubarb, will now feature a 14-year-old protagonist who spends most of his time indoors, binge-watching Netflix and eating Cheetos. When asked for comment, the author merely shrugged and said, "Hey, at least it'll be a change from all those sunburned kids running around in Runt."
