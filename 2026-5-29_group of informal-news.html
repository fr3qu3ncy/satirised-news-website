"DEATH BY PEANUTS: Delhi Workers Trade Comfort for Scorching Heat Just to Afford Snacks"

Delhi's informal workers have decided to voluntarily trade their air-conditioned comfort for scorching sun and potential heat exhaustion in order to earn enough rupees to buy peanuts at the local market, because what's a little dehydration compared to paying rent?

DELHI'S INFORMAL WORKERS: A SURVIVAL STORY

In a bizarre turn of events, Delhi's informal workers have decided to ditch their air-conditioned homes and office spaces and instead opt for the scorching sun outside. Yes, you read that right! These brave souls are trading in their comfortable lives for a chance to feel like they're stuck in a giant oven.

Meet Harish Chandra, a 52-year-old cycle-rickshaw driver who's been pedaling through Delhi's crowded streets for years. When asked why he doesn't just stay home and enjoy the AC, he replied, "What's the point of paying rent if you can't even earn money? My family needs food, man! And besides, my wife and three kids are already at our village in Bihar, living it up in their spacious house with adequate ventilation. I'm like, 'Hey, what about me?'"

Chandra's not alone in his struggles. Mohammad Umar, a 50-year-old tuk-tuk driver, has been working non-stop to avoid missing out on those precious rupees (approximately $5-$7). "If I don't work, my family starves!" he exclaimed, while simultaneously sweating bullets and wiping away tears of desperation.

But it's not all doom and gloom! The Delhi government has come up with a clever solution – heat warnings! Yeah, that's right; instead of providing actual relief, they're just warning people to be careful. It's like telling someone who's on fire to "just be more careful next time."

And if you thought it couldn't get any better, the Prime Minister himself has chimed in with some sage advice: stay hydrated and watch out for signs of heat exhaustion. Because, you know, a few dehydration warnings and some public advisories are all that stand between these workers and certain death.

The International Labour Organization (ILO) estimates that heat stress will reduce India's total working hours by 5.8% by 2030 – or in other words, people will be too busy sweating bullets to actually work. But hey, at least they'll have some quality time to contemplate the meaning of life while stuck in traffic.

In conclusion, Delhi's informal workers are like a bunch of masochists who've decided to voluntarily sign up for a never-ending heatwave. It's a survival story like no other – where workers trade their comfort and sanity for a chance to earn just enough rupees to buy some peanuts at the local market. So next time you're tempted to complain about the heat, just remember: someone out there is willing to sweat bullets for a living.
