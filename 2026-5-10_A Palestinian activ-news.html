BREAKING: Israeli Authorities Deport Two Activists, but Not Before They Get to Enjoy a Comfy Stay in Ashkelon

TEL AVIV, ISRAEL - In a shocking turn of events, two pro-Palestinian activists, Saif Abu Keshek and Thiago Ávila, were deported from Israel yesterday after being detained for trying to sail into Gaza with an aid flotilla. But don't worry, folks, they're not going back to their homes in Spain and Brazil - they're just getting a free vacation in Crete instead.

According to sources close to the activists (or at least, that's what we assume, since we can't actually verify anything), Abu Keshek, a Spanish national of Palestinian origin, was overheard yelling "I'm gonna make some falafel!" during his detention. His Brazilian friend Ávila just shook his head and muttered something about the inedibility of prison food.

The two activists were part of the Global Sumud Flotilla (GSF), which set out to break Israel's blockade on Gaza and deliver aid to the embattled territory. Sounds like a noble cause, right? Well, it turns out that breaking blockades is just not something you can do anymore - nowadays, you have to fill out a bunch of paperwork and get permission from the relevant authorities first.

Speaking of which, Israel's foreign ministry issued a statement saying they had completed their investigation (which we're told was conducted by a team of highly trained hamsters) and confirmed that Abu Keshek and Ávila were indeed being deported. "Israel will not allow any breach of the lawful naval blockade on Gaza," said the statement, which we assume is code for "you can't just sail into our territory without permission, buddy".

Hadeel Abu Salih, a lawyer for Adalah (a rights group representing the activists), claimed that their detention was unlawful and that Israel's accusations against them were baseless. We're not sure what she means by "baseless", but we assume it has something to do with the fact that they didn't have any cash on them.

During their week-long detention in Ashkelon, Abu Keshek and Ávila were subjected to psychological abuse (or so we're told). They were kept in solitary confinement, blindfolded during medical exams (because, apparently, doctors need to see their patients' eyes to diagnose anything), and fed a diet of stale crackers and weak tea. Sounds like the hospitality they received was top-notch!

In related news, the Global Sumud Flotilla has announced that it will be rebranding itself as "GSF: The Original Cruisers" in order to attract more tourists to Gaza.

UPDATE: Sources close to the activists have confirmed that Abu Keshek and Ávila are now enjoying their free vacation in Crete. They've been spotted sipping coffee at a local café, where they're being pestered by aggressive baristas demanding they try some "traditional" Greek pastry.
